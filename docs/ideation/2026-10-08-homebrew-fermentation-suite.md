# Kettlepath: Beer Brewing — on-device recipe-to-brewday suite

## Decision in one sentence

**INFERRED** — The category is homebrewing software, and the job is for an all-grain homebrewer to design a style-targeted beer recipe and then execute its brew-day, fermentation and packaging plan; this is a recipe-and-process suite, not a batch log or a standalone calculator. Evidence: the 16-query Play screen and title hand-reads below; the usefulness of the complete workflow is a product hypothesis.

**INFERRED** — The paying user is a US homebrewer who wants one complete, locally usable recipe and brew-day tool rather than a subscription, and buys the one-time full-suite unlock after trying a limited recipe. Evidence: BeerSmith Mobile is a $4.99 paid listing, Brewfather displays IAP and sells Premium subscriptions, and a critical BeerSmith review complains of lost recipes and missing BeerXML import (market measurements and quote below).

## What is being bought

**INFERRED** — The proposed app is free to install with one editable trial recipe; a **$14.99 one-time Google Play Billing purchase** unlocks unlimited recipe design, water profiles, brew-day runs, fermentation/packaging plans, local persistence, BeerXML interchange and PDF brew sheets. Evidence: this bundle spans six connected work areas; the price is above the sampled $4.99 paid-app ceiling but below Brewfather's official $29.99 annual subscription price. Willingness to pay $14.99 is **UNVERIFIED**; the deciding measurement is paid conversion after release.

**MEASURED** — At $14.99, a US Google Play Billing transaction in the published lower-fee case (10% service fee plus 5% billing fee) leaves $14.99 × 0.85 = **$12.7415**, or about **$12.74 net per sale before taxes, refunds, chargebacks and support**. Evidence: Google Android Developers, “Expanded billing choice and lower fees on Google Play,” https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2026/06/play-expanded-billing.html; this arithmetic assumes the US rate and revenue below $1M/year.

**INFERRED** — The ≥$1,000 monthly net target requires **ceil($1,000 ÷ $12.7415) = 79 paid unlocks/month**, yielding $1,006.58 before the excluded deductions. Evidence: the stated $14.99 price and 15% fee assumption above.

**UNVERIFIED** — Reaching 79 monthly purchases is not established by Play install buckets: Play reported Brewfather at 100K+ lifetime installs, not monthly active users, downloads or purchase conversion, and neither scan reports the size of the reachable homebrewer market. Evidence that would decide reachability: 30/60/90-day funnel readings for installs, trial recipe completion and paid unlocks, plus current active-user acquisition.

**INFERRED** — An illustrative ceiling scenario—not a forecast—is exactly 100,000 category installs × an assumed 1% lifetime conversion × $12.7415 = **$12,741.50 in one-time net receipts**, before excluded deductions; it is not monthly revenue and 100,000 is only the rounded lower bound on Brewfather's installs, not our addressable user count. Evidence: Play enrichment and the explicitly unverified 1% assumption. **UNVERIFIED** — A defensible monthly ceiling cannot be calculated from the available listing data.

## Feature areas: one job from design to packaged beer

1. **MEASURED / INFERRED — Recipe design.** The recipe solver targets a selected style's OG, colour and bitterness ranges, solves grain weights for the brewer's batch size and efficiency, and shows which target is missed when the supplied ingredients cannot meet it. **MEASURED** — The job evidence is `all grain brewing calculator` (scan-market: candidate; title scan: 46/50 on-job) and BJCP style specifications. **UNVERIFIED** — Whether this integrated solver converts is unmeasured.
2. **MEASURED / INFERRED — Boil and hop schedule.** Each hop addition has a timed step and a displayed Tinseth IBU contribution; whirlpool is an explicitly separate steep step and estimate, and dry-hop additions remain in the schedule without being represented as a guaranteed bitterness contribution. **MEASURED** — The `beer recipe designer` query was mixed (9 weak rated incumbents; 23/30 hand-classified on-job); **ON RECORD** — the model and limits are described in “Correctness and formula risk.”
3. **ON RECORD / INFERRED — Brewing-water plan.** The recipe chooses a source-water and style target profile, computes mineral-salt additions and residual alkalinity, and shows a Palmer-model mash-pH estimate with the model named; it warns the brewer to measure mash pH rather than treating the estimate as a lab result. Evidence: John Palmer, *How to Brew*, chapter 15, https://howtobrew.com/section-3/chapter-15/, and Palmer & Kaminski, *Water: A Comprehensive Guide for Brewers*, https://www.brewerspublications.com/products/water-a-comprehensive-guide-for-brewers. **UNVERIFIED** — The exact prediction error for an individual malt lot.
4. **MEASURED / INFERRED — Fermentation and packaging plan.** A recipe carries yeast pitch-rate guidance, fermentation temperature steps, carbonation target and either priming-sugar or keg-pressure instructions into the packaging plan. **MEASURED** — The screened `beer carbonation calculator` query had 12 visible cards but only 3 rated (dead-zone), so this subfeature is not independently demand-proven.
5. **MEASURED / INFERRED — Guided brew day.** The app turns the saved recipe into an ordered, resumable brew-day run sheet with independent timers and scheduled notifications, rather than asking the brewer to keep a batch log. **MEASURED** — `brew day app` was mixed; 25/30 titles were hand-classified as brewing-related, but the visible shelf also included coffee and unrelated brewery apps.
6. **MEASURED / INFERRED — Recipe interchange and brew-sheet export.** Import/export BeerXML through Android's document picker and render a printable/shareable PDF with ingredients, water additions, model/version labels and the run schedule. **ON RECORD** — BeerXML describes itself as a recipe-exchange standard (https://www.beerxml.com/beerxml.htm). **MEASURED** — A sampled BeerSmith review says “No import of beer.xml files.” **INFERRED** — That is one sampled review, not evidence that all BeerSmith users have the limitation.

**INFERRED** — These areas form one calculation chain: style targets and brewer/equipment inputs produce an ingredient and water plan; that plan generates timed boil and fermentation steps; the same resolved numbers populate the live brew-day and packaging instructions and leave the device in a reproducible sheet. Evidence: the six proposed areas above and the BeerXML interchange standard. **INFERRED** — A batch can be represented as a small active run, but browsing/editing a batch ledger is not the app's value.

## Correctness: equations, standard choices and proof

**MEASURED / ON RECORD** — The regression fixture is Brewer's Friend's published **“American IPA” BIAB recipe, recipe 1358407**: 10 L fermentor volume, 15 L boil size, 70% brewhouse efficiency, 2.5 kg Vienna malt (78.1% yield, 3.3 °L), 0.3 kg Caramalt (74.1% yield, 12 °L), 15 g Cascade at 7% alpha for 60 min, and 10 g Strata at 12% alpha for 20 min plus the listed short additions. The published outputs are **OG 1.060, FG 1.015, ABV 5.93%, Tinseth IBU 53.91, Morey SRM 7.18, CO₂ 2.25 volumes**. **MEASURED** — Evidence: https://www.brewersfriend.com/homebrew/recipe/view/1358407/american-ipa, fetched 2026-10-08. **INFERRED** — The fixture must preserve the page's equipment assumptions and exact hop schedule.

**ON RECORD** — The BJCP 2021 American IPA guideline lists OG 1.056–1.070, IBU 40–70 and SRM 6–14; the fixture's published OG, IBU and SRM fall inside those ranges. Evidence: https://www.bjcp.org/style/2021/21/21A/american-ipa/ and the recipe values above.

**INFERRED** — For gravity, solve `OG points = Σ(malt mass × malt potential × brewhouse efficiency) / final batch volume`; for colour, calculate MCU from each grain's mass × Lovibond divided by batch volume, then apply **Morey's** `SRM = 1.4922 × MCU^0.6859`; test the exact 1.060/7.18 fixture outputs within documented rounding tolerance. **MEASURED** — The independent fixture reports “SRM (morey)” and its grain yield, batch volume and OG. **INFERRED** — Precise ingredient potentials and equipment conventions must be held identical in the test data.

**ON RECORD** — Implement **Tinseth** as the named/default boil-hop model because the regression recipe explicitly reports “IBU (tinseth): 53.91” and the original method is discussed in Homebrew Digest #2700, topic “Glenn Tinseth's IBU Calculator,” https://www.hbd.org/hbd/archive/2700.html. **ON RECORD** — The utilization curve is a function of wort gravity and boil time; implement each addition separately and sum contributions.

**UNVERIFIED** — Tinseth, Rager and Garetz produce different IBU estimates; there is no single exact sensory IBU result to promise, and a 0-minute whirlpool steep is not equivalent to a 60-minute boil. The MVP will label Tinseth boil IBU by name, show whirlpool temperature/contact time as a separate estimated steep with its assumptions visible, and show dry-hop timing without claiming a deterministic IBU contribution. Evidence to settle each added whirlpool model: a published model and independently checked test recipes; until then the app must not present that estimate as the Tinseth fixture's measured IBU.

**ON RECORD / INFERRED** — For water, implement the **Palmer/Kolbach residual-alkalinity approach** rather than claim Bru'n Water parity: `RA (as CaCO₃) = alkalinity (as CaCO₃) − calcium/1.4 − magnesium/1.7` when calcium and magnesium are expressed as CaCO₃ equivalents; convert mg/L ion values consistently before using the equation. Use the published Palmer RA / beer-colour relationship for a stated mash-pH estimate and display it as an estimate requiring a calibrated meter check. **ON RECORD** — Evidence: Palmer, *How to Brew*, ch. 15, https://howtobrew.com/section-3/chapter-15/, and Palmer & Kaminski, *Water: A Comprehensive Guide for Brewers*. **ON RECORD** — The `1.4`/`1.7` coefficients apply to CaCO₃-equivalent values; code must not mix those units with raw Ca/Mg ppm.

**UNVERIFIED** — Mash-pH and residual-alkalinity models differ because water profile alone cannot fully encode malt-lot buffering and actual mash conditions; no recon scan measured model error. Evidence that would decide whether the estimator is acceptable: a checked fixture set spanning pale and dark grists, each with published model outputs and actual calibrated mash-pH readings; until that exists, label pH and acid additions as estimates and never promise a guaranteed pH.

**ON RECORD** — Yeast pitch target will use the American Homebrewers Association rate: `target cells = rate × wort volume (mL) × wort gravity (°P)`, with **0.75 million cells/mL/°P for ales** and **1.5 million cells/mL/°P for lagers**. AHA's example is 18,925 mL at 16 °P, yielding about 227 billion ale cells. Evidence: https://homebrewersassociation.org/how-to-brew/make-yeast-starter/ (and the corroborating AHA yeast-trivia page). **INFERRED** — A unit fixture will reproduce that example and separately verify unit conversions; strain viability and starter-growth estimates remain separate, explicitly uncertain inputs rather than hidden corrections to the target.

**ON RECORD** — Bottle priming will use the Brewer's Friend residual-CO₂ equation, `residual volumes = 3.0378 − 0.050062 × T°F + 0.00026555 × (T°F)²`, then compute required fermentable sugar by type as `(target CO₂ volumes − residual volumes) × packaged beer volume × the selected sugar's sourced mass-per-volume-per-volume factor`. Evidence: https://www.brewersfriend.com/beer-priming-calculator/ documents the equation, temperature dependence, sugar types and use of weight; its guidance says to use the highest temperature reached during/after fermentation to estimate residual CO₂. **INFERRED** — Dextrose, sucrose and DME factors must be sourced and checked independently against the calculator's published outputs before implementation; the app will show the temperature and sugar basis, never silently substitute a universal factor. The fixture will include the calculator's 5 US gal, 3.5 oz corn-sugar example (about 2.1 volumes).

**ON RECORD** — Keg carbonation will use Brewer's Friend's force-carbonation temperature/pressure chart, looking up the target CO₂-volume band at the beer's carbonation temperature and returning a clearly labelled regulator-pressure estimate; the source describes the set-and-forget method as approximately 10–14 days. Evidence: https://www.brewersfriend.com/force-carbonation-chart/. **INFERRED** — The implementation will interpolate only between chart cells and test exact published cells plus boundary behavior; it will not present a pressure chart as a kinetic guarantee or promise the time estimate for every keg, regulator or process.

**UNVERIFIED** — These are conventional calculation references, not a guarantee that a particular brewer's actual beer reaches a predicted result. Deciding numerical acceptance requires reproducing the named AHA and Brewer's Friend fixtures within stated rounding tolerances and an independently curated set of measured brew records; residual CO₂ after cold-crashing, yeast viability and growth, actual mash pH, and whirlpool utilization remain model risks.

## Economics and rejected archetypes

**INFERRED** — Proposed archetype: `purchase_led_single_unlock`; capability: Play Billing for one non-consumable full-suite unlock; proposed price: $14.99. Evidence: one durable, self-contained utility has one complete suite to sell once, no server cost or recurring content obligation, and its price is below Brewfather's observed first-year annual Premium price while exceeding the sampled paid-app listing. **INFERRED** — The payment moment is after the user has made or imported the free trial recipe and attempts a second recipe or the complete brew sheet.

**INFERRED** — `purchase_led_repeat_sales` is rejected because the app has no consumable content, paid-generation cost or distinct recipe packs that justify charging the same homebrewer repeatedly; repeat charges would add support burden without measured repeat-sale demand. Evidence: every computation is local and a one-time feature unlock satisfies the described job.

**INFERRED** — `ads_led_utility` is rejected because brew-day users consult the app while handling hot liquid and timed steps; ad interruptions would burden the primary workflow, and no measured ad-impression/eCPM data proves that ads can reach $1,000 monthly. Evidence: this is a workflow judgement and the screened store data supplies no impressions or eCPM.

**INFERRED / UNVERIFIED** — `purchase_only_no_ads` (paid download) is rejected because charging before a brewer can validate the math creates more up-front purchase friction than offering one limited trial recipe; the size of that conversion difference is unmeasured. Evidence that would reverse the decision: a price test showing higher net receipts per install from paid-up-front distribution than from the freemium single unlock.

**ON RECORD** — Subscription is not an archetype option in the factory economics contract; it is banned because ongoing entitlements require subscription state and lifecycle handling. Evidence: `docs/slot-economics.md`, “Subscription is banned.”

## Device capabilities

- **MEASURED — camera: not used.** **INFERRED** — Recipe design and execution need no photographs.
- **MEASURED — photo picker / gallery: not used.** **INFERRED** — BeerXML recipes are structured documents, not photos.
- **MEASURED — media playback or capture: not used.** **INFERRED** — No audio/video is needed to brew the recipe.
- **MEASURED — microphone: not used.** **INFERRED** — No voice input is required.
- **INFERRED — location: not used.** Local source-water profiles are entered or imported by the brewer; location lookup would add no required step.
- **INFERRED — motion sensors: not used.** Tilt readings may be entered manually; sensor integration is outside the proposed first release.
- **INFERRED — contacts / calendar: not used.** The app does not need contacts or calendar access.
- **INFERRED — notifications / reminder: used.** `NotificationManager` / `NotificationChannel` schedules active brew-step and fermentation reminders; without notifications the app cannot alert the brewer when a timer or temperature step is due; manifest/runtime permission: `android.permission.POST_NOTIFICATIONS` on Android 13+.
- **INFERRED — home-screen widget: not used.** The active brew run remains inside the app and Android notifications provide its timed handoff.
- **INFERRED — share / export: used.** `PdfDocument`, `ACTION_SEND` and `FileProvider` share a readable brew sheet; without them the result cannot be handed to another brewer in a printable form; no runtime permission.
- **MEASURED — Bluetooth / USB: not used.** The first release is fully usable without a hydrometer/thermometer; no Bluetooth or USB permission is requested.
- **INFERRED — files / SAF: used.** `ActivityResultContracts.OpenDocument` imports BeerXML and `ActivityResultContracts.CreateDocument` exports BeerXML/PDF; without document access there is no recipe interchange or durable external brew sheet; SAF grants access per chosen file and needs no broad storage permission.

**INFERRED** — Visual identity: a copper-kettle path mark over deep spruce, pale wort and hop-leaf green; custom “brew-step” cards visually connect the current stage to the next, with a persistent numeric target rail that does not look like a stock Material form. **INFERRED** — Evidence: proposed direction based on the brewday workflow. **UNVERIFIED** — Uniqueness versus other future slots requires the owner to compare studio records.

**INFERRED** — Build estimate: **45–60 agent-hours** for recipe domain and test fixtures, BeerXML import/export, the six feature surfaces, local persistence, timer/notification lifecycle, PDF rendering, accessibility/localization, and repeated numerical validation. Evidence: decomposed scope above; this is an estimate, not elapsed work or the factory's one-hour target.

**ON RECORD / INFERRED** — Runtime permissions: `POST_NOTIFICATIONS` only; `ACTION_OPEN_DOCUMENT` / `ACTION_CREATE_DOCUMENT` use user-selected SAF grants, and PDF sharing uses `FileProvider`, so the plan adds no broad storage permission. Evidence: Android platform APIs named in the device declaration.

**INFERRED** — Capabilities required: Play Billing, local database/storage, Android document providers, PDF rendering, timers and notification scheduling; network access is not required for calculations, recipe use or brew-day execution. **INFERRED** — A one-time Play Billing purchase needs the Play Billing capability but no custom backend.

**INFERRED** — Server: none; paid/keyed client API: none. Evidence: all planned arithmetic and timers are on-device; Play Billing collects the non-consumable unlock. **INFERRED** — No subscription entitlement, remote recommendation service or telemetry backend is proposed.

**INFERRED** — Three numeric kill criteria: at **90 days**, fewer than **500 Play installs** or less than **$1,000 net receipts** triggers kill/rework review; the third threshold is fewer than **79 paid unlocks in month 3**, the arithmetic required for the stated monthly target. **UNVERIFIED** — Remaining decision risks are $14.99 willingness to pay, trial-to-purchase conversion, monthly category acquisition, whether the reviewed competitor complaints are representative, import coverage across BeerXML variants, and real-world error bounds for hop steep and mash-pH models.
