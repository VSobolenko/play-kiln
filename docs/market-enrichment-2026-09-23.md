## What this still cannot tell us

- **Install counts are Play's rounded buckets, never counts.** `50M+` means "at least 50,000,000",
  so every size comparison above is a floor comparison. `bolt torque calculator` is the live
  demonstration: three incumbents share `50K+` and this data cannot say which is bigger.
- **A bucket is coarse enough to hide a large difference.** `100K+` spans 100,000 to 999,999, and
  `1M+` spans 1,000,000 to 4,999,999 — so "the leader is at 1M+" does not distinguish a niche app
  from a substantial one.
- **The order inside a query is Play's relevance order, not size order.** The CLI enriches the
  first `--top` cards as Play returned them, and in `bolt torque calculator` the first card is an
  unrated app at `100+`. A larger incumbent can sit below the enriched cut, so "nothing large in the
  top 8" is not "nothing large in the niche".
- **Only 8 of up to 30 visible cards were enriched, and two queries returned fewer than 10 cards at
  all** (`plant disease identifier` 10, `mushroom foraging guide` 9). The rest of the visible set,
  and everything beyond Play's ~30-card display cap, is unmeasured.
- **Revenue, retention and conversion are not observable here.** The store page exposes a badge, not
  a P&L: nothing above says how much PictureThis earns, how many of Migraine Buddy's 67,000 raters
  pay, or what any in-app purchase costs.
- **Whether users would pay for *this* app is not a store-visible fact.** 27/40 apps declaring IAP
  shows the shelf supports in-app purchases in these categories; it does not show that a new
  entrant can convert, and it says nothing about price sensitivity, churn or acquisition cost.
- **A weak rating count does not prove a niche is small.** Play rounds the displayed count and shows
  nothing below a threshold, and an app can simply be new. The counts can also disagree with installs
  in the other direction: `com.shroomid` sits at `500K+` installs with 1,850 ratings while
  `com.mushroom.shroomify` sits at `1M+` with 5,720 — so neither number alone ranks a field.
- **"Ads declared" is not ad load, and "IAP declared" is not price.** There is no ad-network,
  format, interstitial-frequency or price-point data in the HTML this fetcher reads.
- **One storefront, one signed-out session** (`hl=en`, `gl=US`), app concurrency capped at 2 for
  politeness. This is a spot check at the run timestamp above, not a market study.
- **This is not a revenue forecast.** It is a handful of store-visible facts per app — size bucket,
  rating, review count, declared badges, price — and every conclusion above is read from those
  facts only.
